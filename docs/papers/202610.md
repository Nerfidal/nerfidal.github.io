## X-Splat: Unified Gaussian Splatting for Joint RGB and X-ray 3D Reconstruction

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-16 | Wangze Xu, Chu Zhou, Mae Yamaguchi, Linrui Dai, I-Chao Shen, Takashi Ijiri, Imari Sato | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23237v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Jointly reconstructing RGB and X-ray provides new insights by connecting external appearance with internal structure, enabling a more complete understanding of real-world objects than single modality. Yet existing methods train separate models per modality and suffer from redundant geometry, alignment artifacts, and loose coupling that limits cross-modal insight discovery. We propose a unified representation in which all Gaussian primitives share a single set of geometric parameters, with only surface-proximal primitives additionally carrying appearance attributes. We perform cross-modal registration through clean multi-view stereo and X-ray geometric proxies instead of aligning two sets of optimized Gaussians, improving alignment stability. We then jointly optimize the unified representation via supervision from both modalities, complemented by a surface-depth consistency loss derived from X-ray geometry to guide RGB reconstruction. Experiments on the InsideOut dataset and our Flower & Insect dataset show that our framework achieves more robust cross-modal alignment, higher RGB rendering quality without compromising X-ray reconstruction, and a more compact representation than maintaining two independent geometries.

## Frame-Consistent Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from Sparse Unposed Images

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

nerf
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-16 | Viruthshaan Vinasirajan, Ranga Rodrigo | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23039v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Feed-forward Gaussian models have made novel view synthesis almost instantaneous. Feed-forward methods predict Gaussians directly, either in two stages or in a single merged pass. Using two separately trained models is attractive because each can specialize in its own objective: one for accurate pose estimation and the other for high-quality reconstruction, rather than forcing a single jointly trained network to compromise between the two. A natural instantiation pairs a frozen pose estimator, VGGT, with a frozen reconstruction backbone, TokenGS. Prior work, such as VGGT-X and 3R-GS, also adopts a two-stage design, but attributes the resulting quality degradation to noisy pose estimates and introduces learned refinement. We show that this is not the primary source of the problem. Through per-camera analysis across 107 scenes from two datasets, DL3DV and MipNeRF360, using COLMAP poses as the reference, we find that the rotation error after alignment is small (median 1.6 degrees), whereas the translation scale is offset by a large, dataset-consistent factor. Controlled ablations over 140 DL3DV scenes show that the primary obstacles are two integration failures rather than pose error: a coordinate-frame inconsistency and a translation-scale mismatch, both of which can be corrected deterministically. With these fixes, a frozen VGGT driving a frozen TokenGS achieves 22.47 PSNR, within 0.51 dB of the COLMAP reference, with no additional training, when poses are estimated from all scene views. In the sparse setting, where reconstruction uses only four context images under the standard pose-free protocol, the same pipeline matches this all-view quality, yielding a fully COLMAP-free pipeline without any refinement step.

## Direct Conditional Parameterization for N-Dimensional Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-16 | Zhongpai Gao, Benjamin Planche, Van Nguyen Nguyen, Meng Zheng, Anwesa Choudhuri, Arun Innanje, Terrence Chen, Ziyan Wu | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22778v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: N-dimensional splatting extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with view direction and time to model view-dependent and dynamic effects. Each primitive is lifted into a joint distribution over position and conditioning variables, then conditionally sliced at each rendering query. N-DGS and UBS share this step across Gaussian and Beta kernels: recovering the conditional mean, opacity, and covariance requires per-primitive matrix inversions and regression products in every forward pass. We introduce direct conditional parameterization, replacing this covariance-derived readout with explicit per-effect parameters: a Cholesky precision factor for opacity and a factorized query-to-position regression operator with learnable per-dimension coupling. Unrestricted conditional coordinates recover a valid joint covariance; our factorization adds spatial-scale and coupling priors. Applied to both kernels, the parameterization yields dGS (Gaussian) and dBS (Beta). Across five static and dynamic benchmarks, they match or exceed their covariance-derived counterparts on quality, with gains up to +1.26 dB in the main MCMC evaluation. Production slicing is 6.9-7.7 times faster than on-the-fly covariance-derived conditioning; complete-render profiling gives 1.25-1.91 times speedups on four trained scenes. Direct conditioning provides a structured, kernel-agnostic alternative for N-dimensional splatting. Project page: https://gaozhongpai.github.io/dgs/.

## KFS$^4$LAM: Adaptive KeyFrame Selection with Structure-Aware Mapping for Endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-15 | Xinwei Ju, Zhehua Mao, Tianyi Song, Danail Stoyanov, Sophia Bano, Francisco Vasconcelos | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22505v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Gaussian-splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) augments conventional SLAM with a dense, renderable 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality reconstruction and novel-view synthesis. However, its localization, reconstruction, and rendering performance depends strongly on which observations are retained for mapping. Existing systems commonly rely on fixed temporal intervals or simple pose- and overlap-based criteria for keyframe selection. In colonoscopy, non-uniform camera motion, repetitive anatomy, specularities, and visual artifacts can make these criteria unreliable, leading to either redundant keyframes or missed informative viewpoints. We present KFS$^4$LAM (KeyFrame Selection with Structure-aware mapping for endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM), which couples motion-guided adaptive keyframe selection with structurally supervised mapping and restricted temporal deformation. Accumulated motion guides adaptive keyframe selection, with complementary cues suppressing redundant or unreliable candidates. Density-aware mapping with anatomical contour supervision (colon folds) emphasizes structurally informative regions, while restricted temporal deformation accommodates local tissue dynamics without unconstrained geometric drift. We evaluate KFS$^4$LAM against Gaussian-SLAM baselines on C3VD and C3VD-v2, together with cross-backbone and component ablations. KFS$^4$LAM achieves strong overall performance across trajectory, rendering, and depth metrics, with pronounced gains on more challenging C3VD-v2 dataset. In the matched comparison with SplaTAM on C3VD-v2, it reduces rotational relative pose error by 20.3% and depth RMSE by 38.4%, while improving PSNR by 0.78 dB. Cross-backbone experiments further show that the framework is not tied to a single underlying SLAM architecture. Code and visualization results are available at https://xinweiju.github.io/KFSLAM/.

## Compression of Uniformly Distributed Unit Quaternions

{: .label .label-blue }

| 2026-10-15 | Carsten Faber, Bastian Kuth, Rinaldo Schuster, Daniel Pauls, Quirin Meyer | cs.GR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22052v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Unit quaternions are widely used in computer graphics making their efficient compression critical for reducing memory consumption and bandwidth requirements. In this paper, we present a novel method for encoding uniformly distributed unit quaternions. Our thorough analysis of several quaternion parameterizations compares their theoretical error properties, quantization behavior, computational cost, and memory efficiency and provides both an analytical and empirical characterization. We show that an infinity-norm parameterization with our optimized warp function consistently offers the smallest maximum error and the best storage efficiency, at a decompression cost equal to or lower than that of parameterization methods found in practice. We demonstrate the practical benefits of the proposed approach in several graphics applications, including tangent-space normal mapping, skeletal animation with blend weights, and Gaussian splatting.

## ResStreamGS: Residual-Sensitive Dual-Stream Gaussian Splatting for Mobile Volumetric Video Streaming

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-15 | Haoran Liu, Di Wu, Wang Luo, Hengyuan Na, Miao Hu, Guocong Quan | cs.GR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21121v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Real-time mobile volumetric video enables immersive free-viewpoint experiences, but delivering high-quality 3D content under constrained bandwidth and computation remains challenging. Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) systems, such as V$^3$, enable efficient mobile streaming by encoding time-varying Gaussian attributes with commodity video codecs and hardware decoding. However, existing pipelines treat all Gaussians as frame-varying signals, which is poorly suited to full-scene content where large portions remain temporally stable. Our empirical study shows that frame-wise adaptation and video coding of all Gaussians cause quality degradation in complex full-scene content, particularly in temporally stable regions. To address this issue, we propose ResStreamGS, a residual-sensitive dual-stream framework that processes Gaussians according to their temporal behavior. ResStreamGS identifies residual-sensitive primitives through lightweight probe adaptation and reuses stable Gaussians within each Group of Pictures (GOP). Stable Gaussians are transmitted once per GOP, while only residual-sensitive Gaussians remain in the frame-wise hardware-decodable video stream. Experiments on complex full-scene volumetric videos show that ResStreamGS improves post-codec PSNR by 7.51--8.35~dB over a controlled V$^3$-style all-Gaussians baseline, reduces amortized payload by up to 56.5%, and achieves 30.883~FPS on a Qualcomm Snapdragon 778G platform.

**Abstract**: We introduce Edit4D, an editable text-to-4D scene generation framework that constructs 4D scenes from separate background and dynamic character representations. Recent advances in generative models have enabled text-driven dynamic scene synthesis, but editable 4D scene generation remains challenging due to the need to preserve spatial geometry and temporal dynamics. Existing approaches typically generate holistic scene videos, where the background and dynamic characters are coupled, making object-level editing and flexible camera control difficult. ## Anchor3DGS: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting with Compact Anchor-Based Representation

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-15 | Sheng Ye, Jiangke Lin, Fudong Wang, Yi Yuan, Zhen-Hui Dong, Yong-Jin Liu | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21634v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods have enabled effective multi-view 3D reconstruction by predicting Gaussian primitives in a single forward pass. However, existing approaches predominantly adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm that predicts one Gaussian per input pixel, coupling the Gaussian count to the input resolution and number of views. This also leads to redundant primitives in textureless regions and insufficient coverage in geometrically complex areas. We present Anchor3DGS, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that replaces pixel-aligned prediction with a compact anchor-based representation. Guided by information entropy, our method places a set of 3D anchors in the scene, concentrating them in regions of higher visual complexity while maintaining broad spatial coverage. Each anchor performs occlusion-aware aggregation of multi-view image features, exchanges information with other anchors, and is decoded into a set of local Gaussians. A lightweight feature enhancement module further refines the coarse RGB output from Gaussian splatting and improves rendering fidelity. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis quality while only using an order of magnitude fewer Gaussian primitives.

## Training-Free Target-Aware Refinement of 3D Gaussian Language Map

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-15 | Sibaek Lee, Kyeongsu Kang, Seongbo Ha, Yewon Jeon, Joonyeol Choi, Daegeol Ko, Jaehyuk Hur, Taemin Choi, Minsu Kim, Hyungjoo Ahn, Wonsok Yoo, Hyeonwoo Yu | cs.RO | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21619v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Distilling vision-language features into 3D Gaussian Splatting yields a language map that can be queried with arbitrary text. In deployment, however, a small set of user-specified targets must be reliably located, which current distilled maps often miss, while open-vocabulary queryability for other words must remain intact. We propose a training-free, target-aware refinement of an already-distilled language map. Guided by a text-promptable segmenter, the visual embedding inside each detected target region is blended with the target text embedding, a convex combination whose coefficient sets the amount of injected prior, while non-target regions are left untouched. Injecting too much overwrites appearance cues and general queryability, so we seek, for each target, the smallest coefficient that still lets it win, and we obtain every coefficient in closed form, leaving no hyperparameters to tune. We further identify a failure mode of injection in language-goal localization. Text embeddings share a common component, and injecting them makes the standard cosine read-out query-agnostic, which a centering step in the read-out removes. Across three benchmarks, the refinement consistently improves open-vocabulary segmentation, roughly doubling the base mIoU on Replica, and localizes language goals more reliably than the base map. It also transfers as a plug-in to off-the-shelf field-based and object-level language maps and supports a real-world demonstration in which a legged robot navigates to natural-language goals. Project page and code: https://lab-of-ai-and-robotics.github.io/target-aware-langmap/

## GS-PRM: Gaussian Visual Roadmap for Image-Goal Navigation

**Abstract**: Image-goal navigation requires a global planner that connects visually specified endpoints while providing observations that a local policy can follow. Three-dimensional Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides map-registered RGB, opacity, and depth renderings, but does not explicitly encode navigable topology. We introduce GS-PRM, a Gaussian visual roadmap whose milestones, local connections, and visual references are constructed directly through 3DGS rendering without deriving an occupancy grid or signed distance field. ## Feed-Forward Multi-View Feature Coding for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-14 | Seungmi Choi, Ji-Hyoun Choi, Suhyun Jo, Tae-Gwon Park, Hui Yong Kim | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20590v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods enable efficient reconstruction of 3D scenes from sparse multi-view images in a single forward pass, without scene-specific optimization. However, existing feed-forward scene-coding pipelines first generate a complete Gaussian representation and then encode its geometry and appearance attributes separately, often involving non-differentiable operations. The resulting 'generate-then-compress' pipeline requires complex attribute-specific codec designs and limits rate-distortion performance by restricting the exploitation of cross-attribute redundancy. We propose multi-view feature coding for Gaussian splatting (MFGS), an end-to-end feed-forward framework for scene coding. Instead of compressing an explicit Gaussian representation, MFGS extracts multi-view features from the input images and encodes them into a compact bitstream. Through end-to-end rate-distortion training, MFGS learns compact multi-view features that jointly represent geometry and appearance, enabling the receiver to predict Gaussian attributes from the decoded features. We further introduce importance-guided Gaussian selection to accommodate different rendering budgets, allowing the receiver to adjust the number of Gaussians used for rendering from the same bitstream. Across RE10K, ACID, and DL3DV, MFGS achieves 99.76-99.86% bitrate savings over the generate-then-compress pipeline, as measured by BD-rate, while reducing total GMACs by 52-55% and providing an approximately 12 times end-to-end speedup.

## RDCompGS: Rate-Distortion Optimization for Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting with Image Codecs

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-14 | Huiping Ren, Shuai Wan, Luqian Bai, Zhang Chen, Fuzheng Yang | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19538v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis but requires significant storage and transmission resources. Mapping Gaussian attributes to 2D representations enables efficient compression using mature image codecs. However, Gaussian representations that achieve comparable rendering quality are generally treated similarly during optimization, despite substantial differences in coding rate, leading to limited rate-distortion (R-D) performance after compression. To address this limitation, we propose RDCompGS, a 3DGS compression method that enables overall R-D optimization of Gaussian representations with respect to subsequent coding. A differentiable estimate of the coding rate is incorporated into the R-D optimization with rendering distortion. Extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets demonstrate that RDCompGS achieves average Bjøntegaard Delta Rate reductions of 37.84% and 49.31% with H.265/HEVC and JPEG XL, respectively. The estimated rate also shows a strong correlation with the actual bit-rate under different compression conditions.

## HyperLoop: Hyperspectral-Assisted Loop Closure Detection in 3D-SLAM for Planetary Exploration

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-14 | Annie Bhalla, Dennis Rotondi, Riccardo Giubilato, Kai O. Arras, Wolfgang Stuerzl | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19742v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Autonomous navigation of planetary rovers is challenged by visually repetitive, featureless terrain. SLAM systems based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offer dense mapping but rely on RGB appearance descriptors that degrade under perceptual aliasing - a common failure mode in extraterrestrial environments where physically distinct locations appear visually identical. This paper presents HyperLoop, the first framework integrating hyperspectral imaging (HSI) into the loop closure module of a 3DGS-based SLAM system. Hyperspectral descriptors encode material-level spectral signatures complementing RGB features, providing robustness to illumination changes and visual repetitiveness. Two HSI descriptor families are introduced: Bag-of-Spectral-Words (BoSW), encoding spectral material distributions via TF-IDF weighted histograms, and PCA-K VLAD (PCAK-VLAD), capturing first-order residual statistics of PCA-compressed spectral signatures. A key finding is that the sensor's Red-NIR range (600-860 nm) provides limited discriminability for rock terrain: the best HSI fusion (SIFT-VLAD + BoSW, Strict-AND) achieves 5.04 cm ATE RMSE, within 1.8% of SIFT-VLAD alone (4.95 cm). The gain over NetVLAD (8.81 cm) is attributable to SIFT-VLAD, not HSI fusion. This motivates future adoption of SWIR sensors capturing diagnostic mineral absorption features absent from the 600-860 nm range for planetary SLAM missions.

## MicroSDF: Microfacet-Driven Hybrid Neural SDFs for Mixed-Reflectance Surface Reconstruction

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-10-14 | Lejia Ye, Yuhua Xu, Yulan Guo, Lian Xu, Xiaohu Zhang | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19681v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Accurate 3D reconstruction in real-world environments remains a significant challenge due to the coexistence of reflective and non-reflective surfaces, which pose distinct modeling demands. Existing methods often treat these surface types separately, limiting their generalizability and physical plausibility. To bridge this gap, we propose MicroSDF, a novel neural implicit framework that facilitates geometry and reflectance modeling through microfacet theory. Combined with a tailored multi-stage optimization scheme, the proposed MicroSDF achieves robust and high-fidelity reconstruction across reflective and non-reflective surfaces. Extensive experiments on DTU, Shiny Blender, Ref-NeRF, and DeepVoxels datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, establishing a new direction for physically grounded neural reconstruction.

## VisibilitySplat: Ray-Traced Occlusion for Relighting with 3D Gaussian Splatting

| Published | Authors | Category | |

| 2026-10-14 | Sven Kluge, Oliver Staadt | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19847v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: We address inverse rendering with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing explicit visibility modeling for relighting as well as material and light decomposition. Our approach augments the standard rasterized 3DGS pipeline with a sparse visibility oracle that estimates transmittance-weighted occlusion for Gaussian primitives from sampled lighting directions. This oracle is implemented using a point-based ray tracer accelerated by a bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) and queried sparsely within the inverse-rendering objectives, while the original 3DGS loop is retained for training efficiency, interactive visualization, and tooling compatibility. We evaluate our method on the TensoIR synthetic dataset against baselines based on neural radiance fields (NeRFs), tensor representations, and 3DGS. The results show competitive performance and improvements over several earlier baselines for novel-view synthesis (NVS), albedo reconstruction, and relighting. Ablations indicate that explicit visibility modeling is an important contributor to relighting and material-illumination disentanglement, while pure PBR supervision can favor appearance fitting in NVS. Overall, the findings suggest that sparse, point-based visibility queries effectively complement rasterized splatting for inverse rendering while preserving the practical advantages of the 3DGS ecosystem.

New primitives are drawn mostly where screen-space gradients are large. We favor primitives with a low learned confidence, which marks regions the reconstruction still explains poorly, and relocate the primitives that contribute least to the rendered images to these places. Averaged over 24 scenes, our method outperforms 3DGS-MCMC in PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS at every budget from 100k to 2M primitives. At 100k, without a prior or an initialization stage, it recovers 0.84dB of the 1.05dB PSNR by which ConeGS leads 3DGS-MCMC, and from 500k on, it has the highest PSNR and SSIM of all fixed-budget methods we compare with, including ConeGS and EDGS.
