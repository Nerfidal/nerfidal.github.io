## BudgetSplat: Budget-Controllable Generalizable Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-04 | Yuxi Hu, Jun Zhang, Wei Mao, Yuchen Rao, Friedrich Fraundorfer | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06593v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting enables efficient generalizable novel view synthesis, but existing pixel-aligned methods often generate highly redundant Gaussian primitives, causing the final representation size and rendering cost to grow rapidly with image resolution and the number of input views. We propose BudgetSplat, a budget-controllable feed-forward framework that adapts the final Gaussian representation to a user-specified preservation ratio. Rather than treating compaction as simple importance ranking, we formulate it as a joint problem of importance estimation, spatial coverage, and cross-view redundancy. A single trained model supports varying numbers of context views and multiple test-time budgets without per-budget retraining or per-scene compression. To realize this formulation, Intra-Cross Mask Supervision provides structure-, coverage-, and redundancy-aware guidance for learning Gaussian importance, while budget-conditioned feature modulation adapts Gaussian opacity and attributes to the requested preservation ratio. Coverage-Aware Budget Selection further combines patch-wise pre-selection, view-wise opacity ranking, and 3D redundancy suppression to construct compact yet spatially distributed Gaussian representations. Experiments on indoor reconstruction and cross-dataset generalization demonstrate consistent quality--size trade-offs across different input view counts and preservation ratios, substantially reducing final representation storage and rendering cost while preserving rendering quality.

## Feature Puzzles: Single-Image Supervision for Multi-View 3D-Aware Segmentation

nerf
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-04 | Jiahao Ma, Qiang Zhang, Miaomiao Liu, David Ahmedt-Aristizabal, Chuong Nguyen | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05874v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Humans can easily recognize the same objects across different viewpoints of a 3D scene, whereas teaching models such multi-view-consistent perception typically requires videos or calibrated multi-view captures, which are considerably more expensive and less diverse than large-scale monocular data.~In this paper, we introduce Feature Puzzles, a novel framework for learning multi-view-consistent segmentation using only single-image semantic segmentation supervision. Specifically, we propose a simple yet effective pipeline that constructs multi-view-consistent segmentation pseudo-labels through image cropping and geometric transformations. We further introduce a lightweight, plug-and-play network that leverages the multi-view-consistent image features learned by existing pretrained 3D foundation models and the generated pseudo-labels to perform multi-view-consistent segmentation. Importantly, our flexible framework supports various types of prompts provided from one or multiple views and can be readily extended to open-vocabulary 3D segmentation. Under this single-image supervision, our feed-forward model achieves the best mIoU on NVOS, remains competitive on SPIn-NeRF and ScanNet++ against baselines using per-scene optimization or in-domain multi-view training, and demonstrates zero-shot transfer to open-vocabulary 3D segmentation. Visual comparisons are available on our project page: https://featurepuzzles.github.io/

## TwinBridge: Digital Twin-Driven Policy and Reward Transfer for Real-World Reinforcement Learning in Laboratory Robotics

**Abstract**: Online reinforcement learning offers an effective approach to precise laboratory manipulation, yet costly real-world demonstrations and limited access to dense process rewards hinder its practical application. We present TwinBridge, a digital twin-driven framework that supplies policy initialization and dense visual rewards for real-world online learning. We construct digital twins by combining 3D Gaussian splatting with physics simulation. Here, simulator states provide task events and geometric progress under physical prerequisites and ordering constraints, yielding rewards for policy pretraining with simulated demonstrations and reinforcement learning. Experiments show that dense rewards improve interaction efficiency over sparse rewards, and simulation-based policy initialization further accelerates learning.

## Quo Vadis, Driving Scene Reconstruction? Occlusion-Aware Gaussian Densification

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-04 | Dung Do Tien, Kien Nguyen Do Trung, Kiet Dang Vu, Trung Thai Tran, Duc Dung Nguyen | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06273v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: While recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods enable efficient driving-scene reconstruction from surround-view images, their reliance on a single observation fundamentally hinders novel-view synthesis. Regions occluded in the input can become visible from novel viewpoints, leaving the reconstructed Gaussians under-constrained and incomplete. Moving beyond image-space inpainting for rendered views, we introduce OccSplat, a feed-forward framework whose core contribution is Occlusion-Aware Gaussian Densification, explicitly targeting missing scene geometry rather than missing pixels. By predicting new Gaussian primitives for unsupported regions, OccSplat completes the scene directly in the 3D representation. Specifically, OccSplat first identifies occluded regions that lack Gaussian support through Gaussian Occlusion Mapping. Gaussian Occlusion Completion then generates new primitives for these regions. The predicted primitives are integrated into the original representation to improve novel-view synthesis while retaining feed-forward inference without per-scene optimization. Experiments on the nuScenes benchmark demonstrate that OccSplat improves on the compared methods and yields particularly large gains in occluded regions, leading to more accurate and visually coherent driving-scene reconstruction.

- Accepted at the Workshop on Computer Vision for Developing Countries (CV4DC), ACCV 2026 (Proceedings Track)

## METRIC-GS: Selective Metric-Scale Estimation for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-04 | Sooyeun Yang, Jongseong Brad Choi | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06020v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: A 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction inherits arbitrary units from structure-from-motion, so it cannot be measured or used to place AR content until someone supplies a scale. Both standard recipes are fragile on real captures: on five indoor scenes with tape-measured ceilings, a quantile floor-to-ceiling heuristic errs by 27% and metric-depth alignment by 35-71% across three networks. We present METRIC-GS, a selective scale estimator for real indoor photographs: it reads the scale off the reconstruction's own semantic content and, just as importantly, refuses when the evidence is thin. A frozen 2D teacher labels Gaussians by visibility-aware voting; instance-level size priors for architectural and furniture classes (doors, tables, the floor-to-ceiling gap) are fused by a robust maximum-likelihood step; and an inlier-mass test turns thin evidence into an explicit abstention that doubles as a router to a heavyweight metric model. On 47 ScanNet++ scenes with laser ground truth, with a configuration frozen before public evaluation, the estimator alone cuts the heuristic's mean error from 21.1% to 16.6% (9.3% on the 28 scenes it acts on) at ~11 s per scene. MASt3R remains the more accurate standalone estimator (9.9%), but routing only our refusals to it matches running it everywhere with 60% fewer invocations; this selective hybrid is the configuration we advocate. Visibility-aware voting raises the scenes the estimator can act on from 16 to 28 at unchanged deployed accuracy. Applied unchanged to 13 phone and drone captures of our own, on which MASt3R fails its own registration gate 12 times, the system improves all five captures it acts on and degrades none. We also report that a current feed-forward metric model scores 2.3% on a benchmark inside its training mixture and 28-35% on captures it has never seen: a published metric-scale number is a best case, not a deployment estimate.

## CrossPressor: Scalable Cross-View Fusion for Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-04 | Yuxi Hu, Wenhao Dong, Jun Zhang, Friedrich Fraundorfer | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06258v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) becomes increasingly difficult to scale to long multi-view sequences. Anchor-based compression methods such as ZPressor alleviate representation growth by decoding Gaussians only from a compact set of anchor feature maps, but keeping these anchors fixed forces each one to aggregate an increasing number of support views. We identify this representation--fusion mismatch and introduce \textbf{CrossPressor}, a training-free method for scalable anchor-based fusion. CrossPressor preserves all decoder-facing anchor queries while constraining the view-growing support sequence through coverage-cycled evidence routing, distributing retained spatial evidence across support views and fusion layers while keeping all support views represented. Routing before K/V projection, together with joint context packing, further reduces support-side fusion cost without retraining. CrossPressor maintains reconstruction quality close to dense fusion while achieving up to $26.12\times$ faster cross-view fusion and $1.43\times$ end-to-end acceleration on long input sequences. Our project page is at: https://yuhsihu.github.io/CrossPressor.

## GaussianRenderFormer: Transformer-based Rendering of 3D Gaussian Splats


| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Shahaf Valencia Levy, Sagie Benaim | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05593v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Can a neural network learn to render 3D Gaussian splats on its own, with no rasterizer in the renderer? We present GaussianRenderFormer, a transformer that takes a set of Gaussians and a camera and outputs the image directly. Each Gaussian becomes one token in a view-independent encoder, and a view-dependent decoder turns small patches of camera rays into pixels by attending to those tokens. Trained on 26,820 Objaverse objects pruned to 20,000 Gaussians each, GaussianRenderFormer renders 300 unseen objects at 38.08 dB PSNR against the full, unpruned splats, while rasterizing the same pruned input reaches 44.02 dB. The gap is not one of generalization, and our diagnostics are consistent with a bottleneck in the view decoder: telling it the projected footprint of each Gaussian, which it could in principle infer, adds 1.4 dB. Under-training is not ruled out. Code, trained models and videos: https://svlwoof.github.io/gaussianrenderformer

**Abstract**: Purpose: Despite advances in 3D surgical scene understanding, semantic simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM) remains under-explored for two key reasons. First, it requires solving three challenging tasks in parallel: camera pose tracking, high-fidelity deformable reconstruction, and semantic mapping, making progress particularly demanding. Second, the scarcity of datasets providing the necessary ground truth to evaluate all three tasks comprehensively under conditions that closely reflect real surgical procedures environments. Method: We propose EndoS3, a Gaussian Splatting-based semantic SLAM framework that uses instance masks to guide all three tasks. A semantic node allocation module redistributes a fixed node budget across instances according to their size and non-rigidity. We also introduce CRCD++, an ex-vivo Cholecystectomy dataset with RGB-D frames, camera pose and instance annotations for full semantic SLAM evaluation. Results: EndoS3 surpasses all state-of-the-art general-purpose and surgical semantic SLAM methods in tracking, reconstruction and semantic mapping on the CRCD++ and SemSup datasets. Conclusion: Semantic-aware motion and deformation modelling is vital to surgical semantic SLAM. EndoS3 and CRCD++ offer a method and benchmark for improved geometric and semantic understanding in endoscopy.

## 3DGS-VBench+ and 3DGSV-Assessor: A Multi-Dimensional Subjective Benchmark and a Reference-Flexible Objective Model for 3D Gaussian Splatting Video Quality Assessment

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Yuke Xing, William Gordon, Jiarui Wang, Yujie Zhang, Guangtao Zhai, Yiling Xu | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04498v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables real-time novel view synthesis (NVS), but its heavy storage makes compression essential, and both training and compression introduce representation-specific distortions. Since generative compression in many methods treats geometric and color attributes via different parameters, it can induce dimension-specific distortions, motivating multi-dimensional assessment. Moreover, real-world captures generally lack an aligned ground-truth video for a synthesized, continuous camera trajectory, leaving only sparse captured views as references. Extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art correlation with human perception on 3DGS-VBench+ and strong zero-shot generalization to other NVS benchmarks. | 2026-11-03 | Can Wang, Qiyuan Qiao, Lei Liu, Ge Yuan, Xinhui Liu, Dong Xu | cs.RO | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04493v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Learning robust manipulation policies for diverse everyday objects remains challenging because objects with different materials exhibit distinct deformation and contact dynamics. While diffusion policies provide a powerful framework for modeling multimodal manipulation behaviors, they typically lack explicit physical reasoning and may produce averaged actions when facing heterogeneous objects. We present a physics-aware diffusion policy that augments visual imitation learning with object physical understanding. Our framework recovers material properties from visual demonstrations by coupling 3D Gaussian Splatting with differentiable Material Point Method simulation, where video guidance and particle-level constraints enable accurate physical parameter estimation. These results show that incorporating physical knowledge is critical for scalable robot manipulation in diverse environments.

**Abstract**: Spatiotemporal scene reconstruction with Dynamic Gaussian Splatting (DGS) fundamentally depends on perfect temporal synchronization across all cameras, an assumption rarely achievable in practice. **Abstract**: This paper presents GSCV++, a lightweight pipeline for 3D {G}aussian {S}platting (GS) sequences {C}ompression with standard {V}ideo codecs. ## IGSW: Multi-Weather Urban Scene Reconstruction with Intrinsic-Decoupled Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting

| Published | Authors | Category | |

| 2026-11-03 | Zhongquan Wang, Long Cao, Peijia Xie, Senlei Ma, Jie Li, Guofa Li | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04374v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: High-fidelity weather editing of 4D scenes can support end-to-end training and closed-loop simulation for autonomous driving. Combining generative image editing with dynamic Gaussian representations enables multi-weather scene reconstruction, but weather-edited images often contain structural distortions. Directly fitting their appearance may propagate errors from teacher images into the reconstructed 4D scene, compromising the original scene structure and temporal continuity. To address these challenges, we propose IGSW, a two-stage framework that combines intrinsic-decoupled 4D Gaussian scene modeling with structure-preserving weather refinement. In Stage I, background Gaussian appearance is decomposed into weather-shared albedo and normals and weather-specific irradiance and residuals. In Stage II, a structure-preservation gate combines semantic conflicts with cross-weather intrinsic discrepancies to suppress unreliable teacher regions. Per-Gaussian weather transport variables and target-weather branches then strengthen the desired weather appearance over the intrinsic-decoupled scene. Experiments on nuScenes and Waymo show that IGSW achieves the highest content similarity and semantic consistency and the lowest temporal error among the compared methods, while retaining pronounced weather-dependent illumination and atmosphere. ## GSPIM: A Temporal-Locality-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting Accelerator with Polymorphic PIM and Multi-Frame Rendering Dataflow

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Zirui Ma, Zhihua Fan, Haibin Wu, Dongrui Fan, Xiaochun Ye, Wenming Li | cs.AR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04804v2){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) enables high-quality real-time novel-view synthesis for dynamic scenes, supporting free-viewpoint video, mobile XR, and autonomous systems at the edge. Methods using explicit 4D distributions, keyframe interpolation, or anchor binding share two GPU data-movement bottlenecks. Temporal filtering follows model record loading, wasting LPDDR5 bandwidth on inactive data. Frame-isolated execution also overlooks highly overlapping active sets and depth relationships across adjacent frames, reloading shared attributes when working sets exceed on-chip cache capacity. To address these bottlenecks, we present GSPIM, a temporal-locality-aware 4DGS accelerator for PIM-enabled edge devices. Polymorphic PIM adapts a shared near-memory datapath to representation-specific field layouts and selection procedures. It filters and compacts valid data into contiguous inputs before the GPU accesses complete attributes, reducing ineffective transfers. Multi-Frame Rendering Dataflow reuses active attributes and stable depth relationships within a temporal window, reducing repeated work across frames. Hybrid Dataflow Scheduling overlaps near-memory data preparation with GPU rendering to reduce waiting. Evaluation combines a GPU-PIM model calibrated to Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB with a 1z DRAM physical implementation of the added circuits. Across three 4DGS methods and six dynamic scenes, GSPIM achieves a 4.15x geometric-mean speedup and a 4.99x geometric-mean energy-efficiency improvement over a fully optimized frame-by-frame GPU baseline. The maximum scene-level PSNR difference is 0.011 dB, and the added physical footprint is 18.7172 square millimeters across 32 LPDDR5 dies.

## SCARF: A Scene-Adaptive Depth-Guided G-3DGS Encoder Accelerator with Semantic Reuse and Fused Dataflow

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Zirui Ma, Zhihua Fan, Haibin Wu, Wenxing Li, Fulin Zhang, Dongrui Fan, Xiaochun Ye, Wenming Li | cs.AR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02365v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Depth-guided, cost-volume-based Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (G-3DGS) encoders reconstruct scenes as sets of Gaussian primitives directly from multi-view images without requiring per-scene optimization, thereby facilitating feed-forward novel-view synthesis on edge computing platforms. However, their shared encoder architecture presents two primary bottlenecks. First, the per-pixel depth search disregards semantic similarity within the 2D feature space, causing depth search operations to account for over 65% of memory traffic due to redundant exhaustive candidate scans. Second, the sequential pipeline of depth prediction followed by Gaussian generation neglects local 3D geometric continuity, resulting in more than 52% of generated Gaussians being redundant prior to instantiation. In this paper, we introduce SCARF, a dataflow-co-designed accelerator that aligns each bottleneck with a corresponding scene-level regularity. To mitigate the first bottleneck, Feature Similarity Depth Reuse reconceptualizes depth search as a problem of semantic locality: depth consistency is determined by affinity in feature space rather than physical address proximity. It transforms exhaustive per-pixel depth searches into anchor-centered local searches. To overcome the second bottleneck, Scene-Adaptive Early Sparsification disrupts the conventional serial depth-to-materialization boundary by probing each tile to infer local scene regularity. It then selectively activates only the minimally sufficient generation pathway, eliminating redundant Gaussian computations before primitive instantiation. On a 27.8 square millimeter 28 nm post-layout design, SCARF delivers a 2.94x geometric-mean end-to-end speedup with maximum PSNR degradation below 0.072%, 2.23 inferences per joule, and 34.5x GPU energy efficiency.

However, discrete 3D Gaussians usually produce poor depth with few geometry details but lots of floaters, which hinders the effective modeling of smooth radiance fields. To address this issue, we propose to learn a radiance field within a continuous view-dependent color field parameterized by neural networks. With continuous constraints in color, the bias on low-frequency signals of neural networks can highly encourage the geometry modeling to contribute to high-frequency color variations on images, getting rid of solely relying on the color attribute itself. Moreover, we impose a constraint to improve the Lipschitz continuity of the neural color field, making large changes of each Gaussian's color also match with large changes of its position in terms of a ratio. To this end, we additionally introduce novel self-learning strategies to improve the learning of the neural view-dependent color field without any external knowledge or priors, aiming for better generalization on unseen scenes. Our numerical and visual comparisons on widely used benchmarks justify our idea and show better ability of high fidelity geometry recovery in 3DGS. ## Unified Loss-Aware Density Control for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Tae-Young Kim, Jiwoo Chung, Jae-Pil Heo | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02044v2){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: The efficiency and fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting critically depend on adaptive density control, which grows and suppresses Gaussian primitives throughout optimization. However, the standard pipeline still relies on heuristic view-space gradients, scale thresholds, and global opacity resets, rather than explicitly assessing the loss-reducing effect of each split, clone, or opacity update. This mismatch can allocate primitives to already-sufficient regions, miss under-reconstructed structures, and destabilize training through blanket opacity changes. In this work, we recast adaptive density control as a collection of local loss-reduction decisions. We derive efficient Taylor-based per-Gaussian surrogates that predict the objective change caused by three primitive-level operations: splitting, cloning, and opacity decay. This yields a unified loss-aware framework with three components. First, we extend second-order splitting to a normalized joint position-scale space, enabling children to adapt not only their locations but also their anisotropic extents. Second, we formulate duplicate cloning as a surrogate minimization problem, selecting only Gaussians whose duplicated contribution is predicted to improve reconstruction and assigning each child a principled opacity. Third, we replace global opacity reset with selective soft opacity decay that weakens harmful primitives while preserving useful ones. Across standard 3DGS benchmarks, our method produces more targeted primitive allocation and achieves improved rendering quality with comparable or smaller Gaussian budgets compared to baselines.

## AbacusGS: Gaussian Splatting without Per-Splat Sorting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Jiacheng Ding, Yanliang Qi, Cong Guo, Peihua Mai | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01534v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting sorts every splat by depth in every frame. Viewers that draw splats with the hardware rasterizer and sort them on the CPU, as most web and mobile viewers do, spend most of the frame time in this sort. We present AbacusGS, which stores the blending order of a 3DGS scene in the scene layout. AbacusGS groups the splats into octree boxes of at most 128 splats and stores each box in order along an axis chosen at write time. In each frame, AbacusGS sorts only the visible boxes, and one dot product per box decides whether the box is read forward or backward. A short order-aware fine-tuning converts an existing 3DGS model, and the over operator of 3DGS stays unchanged. On 13 standard scenes, AbacusGS loses 0.13 dB PSNR on average against an equally fine-tuned 3DGS model. In an OpenGL viewer that orders splats on the CPU, AbacusGS renders frames 4.5 times faster than per-splat sorting. We also present a compact format of 17 bytes per splat and a CUDA tile renderer that use the same order. The code will be released.

## VLSplat: Vision-Language Guided Object-Centric 3D Gaussian Splatting via Scene Graph

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Yuntae Jeon, Younho Jeon, Sujin Jin, Sungho Jo, Seunghee Park | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01939v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Learning semantic representations for 3D Gaussian Splatting has recently emerged as a promising direction for 3D scene understanding. However, existing 2D-to-3D semantic lifting approaches rely primarily on 2D segmentation supervision and lack object-level priors, causing the learned representation to be driven by local mask evidence rather than holistic object structure. In this paper, we present VLSplat, a vision-language guided approach for object-centric semantic lifting in 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our method augments mask-based lifting with object-level semantic priors derived from vision-language models and refines Gaussian representations using an object-group scene graph. The refinement is performed through two language-guided modules: intra-group refinement suppresses semantic noise within object groups, while inter-group acquisition expands object support regions by incorporating structurally relevant candidates from neighboring groups. Extensive experiments on indoor scene datasets demonstrate that VLSplat improves the semantic consistency and structural completeness of object-level categories. Our code and models are available at https://vlsplat.github.io/.
