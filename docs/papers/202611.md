## GSCV++: Lightweight 3D Gaussian Splatting Sequence Compression via Standard Video Codecs

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Qi Yang, Yingzhan Xu, Kai Zhang, Li Zhang | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05359v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: This paper presents GSCV++, a lightweight pipeline for 3D {G}aussian {S}platting (GS) sequences {C}ompression with standard {V}ideo codecs. To meet the requirements of low-complexity and hardware compatibility from the industry, GSCV++ jointly improves preprocessing, 3D-to-2D mapping, and practical video compression configuration of GS compression. First, for preprocessing, energy-adaptive inter-frame spherical harmonic (SH) alternative current (AC) dimensionality reduction (EA-SHDR) is proposed to produces a compact and stable temporal SH AC feature space. It can reduce the data volume of following mapping and practical compression, as well as less complexity while eliminating redundant information. Second, for 3D-to-2D mapping, prior-based Inter-MiniPLAS (P-MiniPLAS) is designed to generate smooth 2D feature maps through prior-based weighting factor inference and small scale remapping. Compared with vanilla Parallel Linear Assignment Sorting (PLAS) and Morton scan, P-MiniPLAS ensures the generated 2D maps are more suitable for the video codec while maintaining a low level of computational complexity. Third, for practical compression, content-aware generalized compression (CA-GC) is developed to use a non-uniform bit-depth split for coordinate map generation, and a coding unit size selection is introduced for efficient compression complexity control, which fully exploits the performance of video codecs without involving normative changes. Experiments demonstrate that GSCV++ reports over 20\% in intra- and 60\% in inter-prediction rate-distortion gains compared with state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods. GSCV++ also achieves faster 2D map generation, encoding, and decoding than other SOTA methods using the same type of video codecs. Ablation studies verify the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed modules and provide insights for subsequent research.

## IGSW: Multi-Weather Urban Scene Reconstruction with Intrinsic-Decoupled Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Zhongquan Wang, Long Cao, Peijia Xie, Senlei Ma, Jie Li, Guofa Li | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04374v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: High-fidelity weather editing of 4D scenes can support end-to-end training and closed-loop simulation for autonomous driving. Combining generative image editing with dynamic Gaussian representations enables multi-weather scene reconstruction, but weather-edited images often contain structural distortions. Directly fitting their appearance may propagate errors from teacher images into the reconstructed 4D scene, compromising the original scene structure and temporal continuity. To address these challenges, we propose IGSW, a two-stage framework that combines intrinsic-decoupled 4D Gaussian scene modeling with structure-preserving weather refinement. In Stage I, background Gaussian appearance is decomposed into weather-shared albedo and normals and weather-specific irradiance and residuals. Weather teacher images generated by a diffusion-based image editing model and intrinsic maps estimated by a diffusion-based inverse renderer provide feature-level soft supervision for stable structure and illumination changes. In Stage II, a structure-preservation gate combines semantic conflicts with cross-weather intrinsic discrepancies to suppress unreliable teacher regions. Per-Gaussian weather transport variables and target-weather branches then strengthen the desired weather appearance over the intrinsic-decoupled scene. Experiments on nuScenes and Waymo show that IGSW achieves the highest content similarity and semantic consistency and the lowest temporal error among the compared methods, while retaining pronounced weather-dependent illumination and atmosphere. The project page is available at https://akaxi6.github.io/IGSW/ .

## GSPIM: A Temporal-Locality-Aware 4DGS Accelerator for PIM-Enabled Edge Devices

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Zirui Ma, Zhihua Fan, Haibin Wu, Dongrui Fan, Xiaochun Ye, Wenming Li | cs.AR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04804v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) enables high-quality real-time novel-view synthesis for dynamic scenes, supporting free-viewpoint video, mobile XR, and autonomous systems at the edge. Methods using explicit 4D distributions, keyframe interpolation, or anchor binding share two GPU data-movement bottlenecks. Temporal filtering follows model record loading, wasting LPDDR5 bandwidth on inactive data. Frame-isolated execution also overlooks highly overlapping active sets and depth relationships across adjacent frames, reloading shared attributes when working sets exceed on-chip cache capacity. To address these bottlenecks, we present GSPIM, a temporal-locality-aware 4DGS accelerator for PIM-enabled edge devices. Polymorphic PIM adapts a shared near-memory datapath to representation-specific field layouts and selection procedures. It filters and compacts valid data into contiguous inputs before the GPU accesses complete attributes, reducing ineffective transfers. Multi-Frame Rendering Dataflow reuses active attributes and stable depth relationships within a temporal window, reducing repeated work across frames. Hybrid Dataflow Scheduling overlaps near-memory data preparation with GPU rendering to reduce waiting. Evaluation combines a GPU-PIM model calibrated to Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB with a 1z DRAM physical implementation of the added circuits. Across three 4DGS methods and six dynamic scenes, GSPIM achieves a 4.15x geometric-mean speedup and a 4.99x geometric-mean energy-efficiency improvement over a fully optimized frame-by-frame GPU baseline. The maximum scene-level PSNR difference is 0.011 dB, and the added physical footprint is 18.7172 square millimeters across 32 LPDDR5 dies.

## EndoS3:Endoscopic Semantic Splatting SLAM

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Mona Sheikh Zeinoddin, Benjamin Wright, Zafer Tandogdu, Greg Shaw, Evangelos Mazomenos, Danail Stoyanov | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04358v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Purpose: Despite advances in 3D surgical scene understanding, semantic simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM) remains under-explored for two key reasons. First, it requires solving three challenging tasks in parallel: camera pose tracking, high-fidelity deformable reconstruction, and semantic mapping, making progress particularly demanding. Second, the scarcity of datasets providing the necessary ground truth to evaluate all three tasks comprehensively under conditions that closely reflect real surgical procedures environments. Method: We propose EndoS3, a Gaussian Splatting-based semantic SLAM framework that uses instance masks to guide all three tasks. An instance-level motion decomposition module separates static and dynamic instances by evaluating residual flow after compensating for camera motion, accounting for flow noise and comparing motion across neighbouring instances. The camera is tracked against the static Gaussians, while an instance-aware warp field gives each dynamic instance its own control nodes and deformation network. A semantic node allocation module redistributes a fixed node budget across instances according to their size and non-rigidity. We also introduce CRCD++, an ex-vivo Cholecystectomy dataset with RGB-D frames, camera pose and instance annotations for full semantic SLAM evaluation. Results: EndoS3 surpasses all state-of-the-art general-purpose and surgical semantic SLAM methods in tracking, reconstruction and semantic mapping on the CRCD++ and SemSup datasets. Conclusion: Semantic-aware motion and deformation modelling is vital to surgical semantic SLAM. EndoS3 and CRCD++ offer a method and benchmark for improved geometric and semantic understanding in endoscopy.

## Phase-DGS: Phase-guided Dynamic Gaussian Splatting from Unsynchronized Multi-view Video

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Hosung Jeon, Jun Young Jeong, Sangwoon Kwak, Joonsoo Kim, Sangmin Kim, Jaesik Park, Won-Sik Cheong, Hyon-Gon Choo | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04666v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Spatiotemporal scene reconstruction with Dynamic Gaussian Splatting (DGS) fundamentally depends on perfect temporal synchronization across all cameras, an assumption rarely achievable in practice. Industry-standard synchronization requires expensive specialized hardware and complex setup, while data-driven alternatives including audio-based alignment, geometry matching, and pose tracking remain limited by restrictive environmental or scene-specific assumptions. To overcome these limitations, we propose Phase-DGS, a framework that replaces conventional timestamps with a semantic phase representing each frame's inherent state within the underlying motion cycle. Frames capturing the same motion state share the same phase regardless of which camera records them or when, naturally establishing spatiotemporal correspondence across unsynchronized cameras. We validate Phase-DGS under temporal offsets, frame drops, and camera freezes, achieving up to +8.5 dB PSNR improvement over baselines while enabling seamless integration with existing DGS backbones including RealTime-4DGS and FreeTimeGS.

## Physical Diffusion Policy via MPM-Guided 3D Gaussian Simulation

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Can Wang, Qiyuan Qiao, Lei Liu, Ge Yuan, Xinhui Liu, Dong Xu | cs.RO | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04493v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Learning robust manipulation policies for diverse everyday objects remains challenging because objects with different materials exhibit distinct deformation and contact dynamics. While diffusion policies provide a powerful framework for modeling multimodal manipulation behaviors, they typically lack explicit physical reasoning and may produce averaged actions when facing heterogeneous objects. We present a physics-aware diffusion policy that augments visual imitation learning with object physical understanding. Our framework recovers material properties from visual demonstrations by coupling 3D Gaussian Splatting with differentiable Material Point Method simulation, where video guidance and particle-level constraints enable accurate physical parameter estimation. The estimated physics conditions are further integrated into a material-aware Mixture-of-Experts diffusion policy, allowing adaptive behavior generation across different physical regimes. For practical deployment, we introduce a physics memory bank that provides efficient physical retrieval without requiring online simulation optimization. Experiments on a wide range of simulated and real-world objects demonstrate improved manipulation success and generalization over existing diffusion-based approaches. These results show that incorporating physical knowledge is critical for scalable robot manipulation in diverse environments.

## 3DGS-VBench+ and 3DGSV-Assessor: A Multi-Dimensional Subjective Benchmark and a Reference-Flexible Objective Model for 3D Gaussian Splatting Video Quality Assessment

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-03 | Yuke Xing, William Gordon, Jiarui Wang, Yujie Zhang, Guangtao Zhai, Yiling Xu | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04498v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables real-time novel view synthesis (NVS), but its heavy storage makes compression essential, and both training and compression introduce representation-specific distortions. Since generative compression in many methods treats geometric and color attributes via different parameters, it can induce dimension-specific distortions, motivating multi-dimensional assessment. Moreover, real-world captures generally lack an aligned ground-truth video for a synthesized, continuous camera trajectory, leaving only sparse captured views as references. To enable multi-dimensional quality assessment of 3DGS videos with scarce aligned references, we first present 3DGS-VBench+, a large-scale subjective video quality assessment (VQA) benchmark comprising 3{,}036 3DGS reconstructions, each paired with a rendered video, across 11 real-world and 22 synthetic scenes, generated by 6 representative compression algorithms at systematically designed levels and annotated with 9{,}108 MOSs covering overall, geometry, and color quality. Based on 3DGS-VBench+, we propose 3DGSV-Assessor, a reference-flexible, large multimodal framework that scores all three dimensions in one generation pass and fuses a ranking-based reference cue into the frozen regressor through a bounded gate, supporting both reduced-reference (RR) and no-reference (NR) assessment. Extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art correlation with human perception on 3DGS-VBench+ and strong zero-shot generalization to other NVS benchmarks. The dataset and code will be released at https://github.com/YukeXing/3DGSV-Assessor.

## 3D Gaussian Splatting within Self-Learned Neural View-Dependent Color Fields

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Yixiao Yang, Yu-Shen Liu, Zhizhong Han | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03114v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a vital tool in novel view synthesis. 3D Gaussian functions with attributes like color, opacity, and scale are stacked together to explicitly model a radiance field. However, discrete 3D Gaussians usually produce poor depth with few geometry details but lots of floaters, which hinders the effective modeling of smooth radiance fields. To address this issue, we propose to learn a radiance field within a continuous view-dependent color field parameterized by neural networks. With continuous constraints in color, the bias on low-frequency signals of neural networks can highly encourage the geometry modeling to contribute to high-frequency color variations on images, getting rid of solely relying on the color attribute itself. Moreover, we impose a constraint to improve the Lipschitz continuity of the neural color field, making large changes of each Gaussian's color also match with large changes of its position in terms of a ratio. To this end, we additionally introduce novel self-learning strategies to improve the learning of the neural view-dependent color field without any external knowledge or priors, aiming for better generalization on unseen scenes. Our numerical and visual comparisons on widely used benchmarks justify our idea and show better ability of high fidelity geometry recovery in 3DGS. Project page: https://github.com/yangyixi21/Neural-Color-Fields-3DGS.

## GaussFlaw: Fine-Grained Perceptual Diagnosis for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Shaocheng Shen, Zihan Zheng, Kejin Li, Lu Liu, Xiaoyun Zhang, Qiang Hu, Wenjun Zhang | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02767v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables photorealistic novel- view synthesis with real-time rendering, yet fine-grained di- agnosis of its rendering artifacts remains challenging. These artifacts are typically spatially localized and exhibit diverse degradation patterns, making them difficult to character- ize using global quality metrics and image-level assessment benchmarks, which provide no explicit indication of where they occur or what types of artifacts they represent. To ad- dress this limitation, we introduce GaussFlaw-80K, the first large-scale dataset for fine-grained perceptual diagnosis in 3DGS. Constructed through a model-assisted human-in-the- loop pipeline, GaussFlaw-80K contains 80,049 artifact re- gions across 14,289 rendered images, each annotated with a pixel-level mask and one of eight flaw labels spanning ge- ometric, blending, and photometric degradations. These an- notations turn 3DGS artifact diagnosis into an explicit and measurable task. Building on this dataset, we propose Ge- oCritic, a geometry-guided multi-task model that integrates RGB appearance with depth-derived cues to jointly localize artifacts and classify their flaw types. Beyond evaluation, we further demonstrate the downstream benefits of fine-grained diagnosis through three complementary targeted operations: Gaussian densification, attribute correction, and outlier prun- ing. Experiments show that GeoCritic achieves an AUC-J of 0.9507 and an Acc@1 of 80.08%, while diagnosis-guided re- finement improves all reported global and crop-level metrics across diverse datasets and 3DGS backbones.

## OctreeSplat: Hierarchical Octree-Guided Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Yibin Zhao, Yihan Pan, Wenli Yang, Jun Nan, Jianjun Yi | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02658v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has shown considerable potential for novel view synthesis (NVS) and 3D reconstruction. Feed-forward methods avoid costly per-scene optimization, but fixed image or spatial resolutions limit their ability to allocate primitives according to local complexity. We present OctreeSplat, a hierarchical octree-guided feed-forward Gaussian splatting. OctreeSplat extracts multi-view features and geometric priors from a frozen visual foundation model (VFM), constructs a hierarchical octree, and predicts densification scores. The resulting representation allocates Gaussian primitives at spatially varying densities according to local complexity. Stochastic solids volume rendering improves depth rendering, strengthens geometric supervision, and supports surface reconstruction. When camera parameters are available, an optional test-time optimization (TTO) module improves agreement between predicted and ground-truth parameters. Training updates only 76 million additional parameters and requires 35 GPU-hours. Experiments on Replica, TUM, Tanks and Temples, and DTU demonstrate competitive RGB view synthesis, depth synthesis, and surface reconstruction. OctreeSplat reduces the number of Gaussian primitives while maintaining high reconstruction quality.

## SCARF: A Scene-Adaptive Depth-Guided G-3DGS Encoder Accelerator with Semantic Reuse and Fused Dataflow

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Zirui Ma, Zhihua Fan, Haibin Wu, Wenxing Li, Fulin Zhang, Dongrui Fan, Xiaochun Ye, Wenming Li | cs.AR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02365v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Depth-guided, cost-volume-based Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (G-3DGS) encoders reconstruct scenes as sets of Gaussian primitives directly from multi-view images without requiring per-scene optimization, thereby facilitating feed-forward novel-view synthesis on edge computing platforms. However, their shared encoder architecture presents two primary bottlenecks. First, the per-pixel depth search disregards semantic similarity within the 2D feature space, causing depth search operations to account for over 65% of memory traffic due to redundant exhaustive candidate scans. Second, the sequential pipeline of depth prediction followed by Gaussian generation neglects local 3D geometric continuity, resulting in more than 52% of generated Gaussians being redundant prior to instantiation. In this paper, we introduce SCARF, a dataflow-co-designed accelerator that aligns each bottleneck with a corresponding scene-level regularity. To mitigate the first bottleneck, Feature Similarity Depth Reuse reconceptualizes depth search as a problem of semantic locality: depth consistency is determined by affinity in feature space rather than physical address proximity. It transforms exhaustive per-pixel depth searches into anchor-centered local searches. To overcome the second bottleneck, Scene-Adaptive Early Sparsification disrupts the conventional serial depth-to-materialization boundary by probing each tile to infer local scene regularity. It then selectively activates only the minimally sufficient generation pathway, eliminating redundant Gaussian computations before primitive instantiation. On a 27.8 square millimeter 28 nm post-layout design, SCARF delivers a 2.94x geometric-mean end-to-end speedup with maximum PSNR degradation below 0.072%, 2.23 inferences per joule, and 34.5x GPU energy efficiency.

## Unified Loss-Aware Density Control for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Tae-Young Kim, Jiwoo Chung, Jae-Pil Heo | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02044v2){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: The efficiency and fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting critically depend on adaptive density control, which grows and suppresses Gaussian primitives throughout optimization. However, the standard pipeline still relies on heuristic view-space gradients, scale thresholds, and global opacity resets, rather than explicitly assessing the loss-reducing effect of each split, clone, or opacity update. This mismatch can allocate primitives to already-sufficient regions, miss under-reconstructed structures, and destabilize training through blanket opacity changes. In this work, we recast adaptive density control as a collection of local loss-reduction decisions. We derive efficient Taylor-based per-Gaussian surrogates that predict the objective change caused by three primitive-level operations: splitting, cloning, and opacity decay. This yields a unified loss-aware framework with three components. First, we extend second-order splitting to a normalized joint position-scale space, enabling children to adapt not only their locations but also their anisotropic extents. Second, we formulate duplicate cloning as a surrogate minimization problem, selecting only Gaussians whose duplicated contribution is predicted to improve reconstruction and assigning each child a principled opacity. Third, we replace global opacity reset with selective soft opacity decay that weakens harmful primitives while preserving useful ones. Across standard 3DGS benchmarks, our method produces more targeted primitive allocation and achieves improved rendering quality with comparable or smaller Gaussian budgets compared to baselines.

- Accepted to NeurIPS 2026

## AbacusGS: Gaussian Splatting without Per-Splat Sorting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Jiacheng Ding, Yanliang Qi, Cong Guo, Peihua Mai | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01534v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting sorts every splat by depth in every frame. Viewers that draw splats with the hardware rasterizer and sort them on the CPU, as most web and mobile viewers do, spend most of the frame time in this sort. We present AbacusGS, which stores the blending order of a 3DGS scene in the scene layout. AbacusGS groups the splats into octree boxes of at most 128 splats and stores each box in order along an axis chosen at write time. In each frame, AbacusGS sorts only the visible boxes, and one dot product per box decides whether the box is read forward or backward. A short order-aware fine-tuning converts an existing 3DGS model, and the over operator of 3DGS stays unchanged. On 13 standard scenes, AbacusGS loses 0.13 dB PSNR on average against an equally fine-tuned 3DGS model. In an OpenGL viewer that orders splats on the CPU, AbacusGS renders frames 4.5 times faster than per-splat sorting. We also present a compact format of 17 bytes per splat and a CUDA tile renderer that use the same order. The code will be released.

- 10 pages, 5 figures, 6 tables

## VLSplat: Vision-Language Guided Object-Centric 3D Gaussian Splatting via Scene Graph

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Yuntae Jeon, Younho Jeon, Sujin Jin, Sungho Jo, Seunghee Park | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01939v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Learning semantic representations for 3D Gaussian Splatting has recently emerged as a promising direction for 3D scene understanding. However, existing 2D-to-3D semantic lifting approaches rely primarily on 2D segmentation supervision and lack object-level priors, causing the learned representation to be driven by local mask evidence rather than holistic object structure. In this paper, we present VLSplat, a vision-language guided approach for object-centric semantic lifting in 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our method augments mask-based lifting with object-level semantic priors derived from vision-language models and refines Gaussian representations using an object-group scene graph. The refinement is performed through two language-guided modules: intra-group refinement suppresses semantic noise within object groups, while inter-group acquisition expands object support regions by incorporating structurally relevant candidates from neighboring groups. Extensive experiments on indoor scene datasets demonstrate that VLSplat improves the semantic consistency and structural completeness of object-level categories. Our code and models are available at https://vlsplat.github.io/.
