## 3D Gaussian Splatting within Self-Learned Neural View-Dependent Color Fields

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Yixiao Yang, Yu-Shen Liu, Zhizhong Han | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03114v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a vital tool in novel view synthesis. 3D Gaussian functions with attributes like color, opacity, and scale are stacked together to explicitly model a radiance field. However, discrete 3D Gaussians usually produce poor depth with few geometry details but lots of floaters, which hinders the effective modeling of smooth radiance fields. To address this issue, we propose to learn a radiance field within a continuous view-dependent color field parameterized by neural networks. With continuous constraints in color, the bias on low-frequency signals of neural networks can highly encourage the geometry modeling to contribute to high-frequency color variations on images, getting rid of solely relying on the color attribute itself. Moreover, we impose a constraint to improve the Lipschitz continuity of the neural color field, making large changes of each Gaussian's color also match with large changes of its position in terms of a ratio. To this end, we additionally introduce novel self-learning strategies to improve the learning of the neural view-dependent color field without any external knowledge or priors, aiming for better generalization on unseen scenes. Our numerical and visual comparisons on widely used benchmarks justify our idea and show better ability of high fidelity geometry recovery in 3DGS. Project page: https://github.com/yangyixi21/Neural-Color-Fields-3DGS.

## GaussFlaw: Fine-Grained Perceptual Diagnosis for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Shaocheng Shen, Zihan Zheng, Kejin Li, Lu Liu, Xiaoyun Zhang, Qiang Hu, Wenjun Zhang | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02767v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables photorealistic novel- view synthesis with real-time rendering, yet fine-grained di- agnosis of its rendering artifacts remains challenging. These artifacts are typically spatially localized and exhibit diverse degradation patterns, making them difficult to character- ize using global quality metrics and image-level assessment benchmarks, which provide no explicit indication of where they occur or what types of artifacts they represent. To ad- dress this limitation, we introduce GaussFlaw-80K, the first large-scale dataset for fine-grained perceptual diagnosis in 3DGS. Constructed through a model-assisted human-in-the- loop pipeline, GaussFlaw-80K contains 80,049 artifact re- gions across 14,289 rendered images, each annotated with a pixel-level mask and one of eight flaw labels spanning ge- ometric, blending, and photometric degradations. These an- notations turn 3DGS artifact diagnosis into an explicit and measurable task. Building on this dataset, we propose Ge- oCritic, a geometry-guided multi-task model that integrates RGB appearance with depth-derived cues to jointly localize artifacts and classify their flaw types. Beyond evaluation, we further demonstrate the downstream benefits of fine-grained diagnosis through three complementary targeted operations: Gaussian densification, attribute correction, and outlier prun- ing. Experiments show that GeoCritic achieves an AUC-J of 0.9507 and an Acc@1 of 80.08%, while diagnosis-guided re- finement improves all reported global and crop-level metrics across diverse datasets and 3DGS backbones.

## OctreeSplat: Hierarchical Octree-Guided Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Yibin Zhao, Yihan Pan, Wenli Yang, Jun Nan, Jianjun Yi | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02658v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has shown considerable potential for novel view synthesis (NVS) and 3D reconstruction. Feed-forward methods avoid costly per-scene optimization, but fixed image or spatial resolutions limit their ability to allocate primitives according to local complexity. We present OctreeSplat, a hierarchical octree-guided feed-forward Gaussian splatting. OctreeSplat extracts multi-view features and geometric priors from a frozen visual foundation model (VFM), constructs a hierarchical octree, and predicts densification scores. The resulting representation allocates Gaussian primitives at spatially varying densities according to local complexity. Stochastic solids volume rendering improves depth rendering, strengthens geometric supervision, and supports surface reconstruction. When camera parameters are available, an optional test-time optimization (TTO) module improves agreement between predicted and ground-truth parameters. Training updates only 76 million additional parameters and requires 35 GPU-hours. Experiments on Replica, TUM, Tanks and Temples, and DTU demonstrate competitive RGB view synthesis, depth synthesis, and surface reconstruction. OctreeSplat reduces the number of Gaussian primitives while maintaining high reconstruction quality.

## SCARF: A Scene-Adaptive Depth-Guided G-3DGS Encoder Accelerator with Semantic Reuse and Fused Dataflow

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-02 | Zirui Ma, Zhihua Fan, Haibin Wu, Wenxing Li, Fulin Zhang, Dongrui Fan, Xiaochun Ye, Wenming Li | cs.AR | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02365v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Depth-guided, cost-volume-based Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (G-3DGS) encoders reconstruct scenes as sets of Gaussian primitives directly from multi-view images without requiring per-scene optimization, thereby facilitating feed-forward novel-view synthesis on edge computing platforms. However, their shared encoder architecture presents two primary bottlenecks. First, the per-pixel depth search disregards semantic similarity within the 2D feature space, causing depth search operations to account for over 65% of memory traffic due to redundant exhaustive candidate scans. Second, the sequential pipeline of depth prediction followed by Gaussian generation neglects local 3D geometric continuity, resulting in more than 52% of generated Gaussians being redundant prior to instantiation. In this paper, we introduce SCARF, a dataflow-co-designed accelerator that aligns each bottleneck with a corresponding scene-level regularity. To mitigate the first bottleneck, Feature Similarity Depth Reuse reconceptualizes depth search as a problem of semantic locality: depth consistency is determined by affinity in feature space rather than physical address proximity. It transforms exhaustive per-pixel depth searches into anchor-centered local searches. To overcome the second bottleneck, Scene-Adaptive Early Sparsification disrupts the conventional serial depth-to-materialization boundary by probing each tile to infer local scene regularity. It then selectively activates only the minimally sufficient generation pathway, eliminating redundant Gaussian computations before primitive instantiation. On a 27.8 square millimeter 28 nm post-layout design, SCARF delivers a 2.94x geometric-mean end-to-end speedup with maximum PSNR degradation below 0.072%, 2.23 inferences per joule, and 34.5x GPU energy efficiency.

- 17 pages, 16 figures. Accepted by MICRO 2026; awarded all three MICRO 2026 Artifact Evaluation badges. Code repo: https://github.com/MAdrid1011/SCARF

## AbacusGS: Gaussian Splatting without Per-Splat Sorting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Jiacheng Ding, Yanliang Qi, Cong Guo, Peihua Mai | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01534v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: 3D Gaussian Splatting sorts every splat by depth in every frame. Viewers that draw splats with the hardware rasterizer and sort them on the CPU, as most web and mobile viewers do, spend most of the frame time in this sort. We present AbacusGS, which stores the blending order of a 3DGS scene in the scene layout. AbacusGS groups the splats into octree boxes of at most 128 splats and stores each box in order along an axis chosen at write time. In each frame, AbacusGS sorts only the visible boxes, and one dot product per box decides whether the box is read forward or backward. A short order-aware fine-tuning converts an existing 3DGS model, and the over operator of 3DGS stays unchanged. On 13 standard scenes, AbacusGS loses 0.13 dB PSNR on average against an equally fine-tuned 3DGS model. In an OpenGL viewer that orders splats on the CPU, AbacusGS renders frames 4.5 times faster than per-splat sorting. We also present a compact format of 17 bytes per splat and a CUDA tile renderer that use the same order. The code will be released.

- 10 pages, 5 figures, 6 tables

## Unified Loss-Aware Density Control for 3D Gaussian Splatting

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Tae-Young Kim, Jiwoo Chung, Jae-Pil Heo | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02044v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: The efficiency and fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting critically depend on adaptive density control, which grows and suppresses Gaussian primitives throughout optimization. However, the standard pipeline still relies on heuristic view-space gradients, scale thresholds, and global opacity resets, rather than explicitly assessing the loss-reducing effect of each split, clone, or opacity update. This mismatch can allocate primitives to already-sufficient regions, miss under-reconstructed structures, and destabilize training through blanket opacity changes. In this work, we recast adaptive density control as a collection of local loss-reduction decisions. We derive efficient Taylor-based per-Gaussian surrogates that predict the objective change caused by three primitive-level operations: splitting, cloning, and opacity decay. This yields a unified loss-aware framework with three components. First, we extend second-order splitting to a normalized joint position-scale space, enabling children to adapt not only their locations but also their anisotropic extents. Second, we formulate duplicate cloning as a surrogate minimization problem, selecting only Gaussians whose duplicated contribution is predicted to improve reconstruction and assigning each child a principled opacity. Third, we replace global opacity reset with selective soft opacity decay that weakens harmful primitives while preserving useful ones. Across standard 3DGS benchmarks, our method produces more targeted primitive allocation and achieves improved rendering quality with comparable or smaller Gaussian budgets compared to baselines.

- Accepted to NeurIPS 2026

## VLSplat: Vision-Language Guided Object-Centric 3D Gaussian Splatting via Scene Graph

gaussian splatting
{: .label .label-blue }

| Published | Authors | Category | |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2026-11-01 | Yuntae Jeon, Younho Jeon, Sujin Jin, Sungho Jo, Seunghee Park | cs.CV | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01939v1){: .btn .btn-green } |

**Abstract**: Learning semantic representations for 3D Gaussian Splatting has recently emerged as a promising direction for 3D scene understanding. However, existing 2D-to-3D semantic lifting approaches rely primarily on 2D segmentation supervision and lack object-level priors, causing the learned representation to be driven by local mask evidence rather than holistic object structure. In this paper, we present VLSplat, a vision-language guided approach for object-centric semantic lifting in 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our method augments mask-based lifting with object-level semantic priors derived from vision-language models and refines Gaussian representations using an object-group scene graph. The refinement is performed through two language-guided modules: intra-group refinement suppresses semantic noise within object groups, while inter-group acquisition expands object support regions by incorporating structurally relevant candidates from neighboring groups. Extensive experiments on indoor scene datasets demonstrate that VLSplat improves the semantic consistency and structural completeness of object-level categories. Our code and models are available at https://vlsplat.github.io/.

- Accepted to NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://vlsplat.github.io/
